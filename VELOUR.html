The Founding Story

"A man's wardrobe is not assembled. It is accumulated — piece by deliberate piece."
— Ghulam Hussain, Founder, 1948

On a monsoon morning in August 1948, Ghulam Hussain opened a single-room tailor shop at 14 Anarkali Bazaar, Lahore. He had apprenticed for twelve years — six in Lahore, three in Bombay, three in London — before cutting his first coat under his own name.

He made one rule: every garment would be fully canvassed and hand-stitched. Not because it was necessary. Because it was right. That rule has never changed.

Today, his grandson Imran Hussain runs the atelier. The address is the same. The shears are different. The rule is not.

Ghulam Hussain

The Journey

Our History

1948
The First Cut
Ghulam Hussain opens at 14 Anarkali Bazaar. His first commission: a three-piece suit for a civil servant. It takes eleven days. The client wears it for thirty years.
1962
The Second Generation
Ghulam's son Tariq Hussain joins at age sixteen. He introduces wool sourcing from English mills — the first Pakistani tailor to do so directly.
1979
The Velour Name
The brand adopts the name VELOUR HOMME. The wordmark is designed by a Lahori calligrapher and has not changed since.
1998
The Third Generation
Imran Hussain, trained in Naples under master tailor Gennaro Rubinacci, returns to Lahore. He brings Neapolitan construction to the Lahore tradition.
2025
Now
VELOUR HOMME launches its first digital presence. Nothing else changes. Every garment still fully canvassed and hand-stitched.
The Craft

How We Build

◈
Full Canvas Construction
Every suit jacket is built over a full canvas — a floating internal structure of horsehair and linen that moulds to the wearer's body over time. No fusing. No shortcuts.
✦
Hand-Padded Lapels
Our lapels are hand-padded over two days — thousands of tiny stitches that create a roll which no fused lapel can replicate.
◇
Heritage Fabrics
We source from Scabal and Dormeuil in London, Loro Piana in Italy, and our own Punjab cotton mills. Every bolt inspected by hand.
○
Hand-Pressed Seams
Every seam in every garment is pressed by hand — once before sewing, once after. A steam iron and a wooden clapper.
▲
Working Buttonholes
Our cuff buttonholes are functional — cut by hand with a scalpel, bound with silk thread. A detail felt only by a man who knows what to look for.
✦
Lifetime Guarantee
Every garment carries a lifetime guarantee on construction. If a seam opens, we repair it — without question, without charge, without exception.
